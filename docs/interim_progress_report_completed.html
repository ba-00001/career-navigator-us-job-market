Section 1 - Description of Data

Summary of the Data

This project uses a multi-table labor-market dataset assembled from official U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and O*NET sources. The goal is to analyze the U.S. entry-level job market across sectors so the team can identify where early-career workers are most likely to find strong opportunities, what occupations are growing fastest, how wages vary across sectors, and how job characteristics differ by occupation and industry.

The final business framing connects the analysis to the concept of a LinkedIn Career Navigator AI that could help users choose sectors, occupations, and learning paths based on labor-market evidence.

Data Sources Being Used

  1. BLS Employment Projections tables
    • Occupational projections and worker characteristics
    • Industry-occupation matrix data
    • Occupational openings and separations
    • Employment and output by industry
  2. BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics tables
    • National industry-specific and by ownership
    • All data tables
  3. BLS State and Area Employment annual average tables
    • Employees on nonfarm payrolls in states and selected areas by major industry
  4. BLS Labor Force Characteristics tables
    • Average hours at work by industry
    • Average hours at work by occupation
  5. O*NET occupation attributes
    • Work Activities.xlsx

Why This Data Fits the Project

This dataset is a strong fit for the group project because it is real, multi-table, and suitable for Tableau-based business analysis. It supports data preparation, joins, calculated fields, clustering, forecasting, dashboards, and storytelling. It also allows the project to compare sectors and occupations using multiple criteria instead of only one measure such as wage.

Data Preparation Plan

  1. Standardize occupation codes across BLS and O*NET so occupation-level joins work correctly.
  2. Standardize sector and industry labels so wages, growth, employment, and openings can be compared consistently.
  3. Filter for entry-level occupations using education, training, and related-work-experience fields.
  4. Create calculated fields such as entry-level flag, growth category, wage percentile group, accessibility score, and attractiveness score.
  5. Handle missing, suppressed, or non-comparable values through filtering, null handling, or documented exclusions.
  6. Join occupation-level, industry-level, and geography-level tables using occupation code, industry code, and state fields where appropriate.
  7. Reshape long-format data such as O*NET work activities when needed for Tableau visuals.
  8. Build focused analysis-ready subsets for occupations, sectors, state-sector employment, occupation attributes, and occupation-sector mapping.

Section 2 - Purpose of the Analysis

Overall Purpose

The purpose of the analysis is to identify where the strongest entry-level opportunities exist in the U.S. job market and how those opportunities vary by sector, occupation, wages, projected growth, education requirements, and work characteristics. The group will use the analysis to support the concept of a LinkedIn Career Navigator AI that helps early-career users choose sectors and occupations using real market data.

Angle of the Analysis

The most interesting angle is not simply which jobs pay the most. Instead, the team will evaluate opportunity using a combination of accessibility for entry-level workers, projected growth and openings, sector demand, wage potential, work-activity patterns, and geography. This gives the project a stronger business story and makes the Tableau output more useful for practical career decision-making.

Specific Questions That Will Guide the Analysis

  1. Which U.S. sectors currently offer the strongest volume of employment for entry-level workers?
  2. Which entry-level occupations have the highest projected growth and annual openings?
  3. How do wages for similar entry-level occupations differ across sectors?
  4. Which sectors combine stronger wage potential with stronger projected employment growth?
  5. How do education and training requirements vary across entry-level occupations and sectors?
  6. Which states show the strongest concentration of employment in sectors that appear attractive for early-career workers?
  7. Can occupations be clustered into meaningful groups based on work activities and job characteristics?
  8. Which occupations appear most attractive when balancing accessibility, wages, and growth?

Planned Tableau Story Direction

To keep the project manageable while still showing analytical depth, the Tableau story will focus on major U.S. sectors, entry-level occupations, selected high-interest sectors such as healthcare, professional services, retail, logistics, finance, and technology-related services, and occupation clusters built from work-activity patterns.

A strong final narrative for the project is: "Where should an early-career worker start? A data-driven look at entry-level opportunities across U.S. sectors."
